  \caption{\parbox[t]{\dimexpr\linewidth-4.5em}{MadSpin unweighting cost per accept/reject stage for $p\,p \to t\bar t$ with both tops decayed ($t \to b\,W^+$, $W^+ \to \ell^+\nu$ and charge conjugate), before and after the per-event mass-stage bound. One production sample of 100\,000 events and one MadSpin seed are shared by every row \emph{and by both campaigns}, so the two halves of each row differ only by the code under test. Each $\epsilon$ is the average number of generated points needed to accept one, so \emph{lower is better and the floor is~1}: $\epsilon_m$ counts virtuality (mass) sets drawn per written event, $\epsilon_t$ and $\epsilon_{\bar t}$ count decay events drawn for the $t$ and $\bar t$ slot per written event, and $N_{\mathrm{dec}}$ is the total number of decay events consumed over the whole run. \texttt{joint} has no separate phases -- it makes a single accept/reject over the virtualities and both decays at once -- so it yields one efficiency, spanning the three $\epsilon$ columns. \texttt{onshell} keeps the production kinematics and never samples a virtuality, so it has no mass stage at all and $\epsilon_m$ is not defined there (dash, not~1); under \texttt{sequential\_global\_retry} MadSpin still prints a ``mass stage'' line, but what it counts there is chain restarts with no mass in them (hence a number identical to the slot-0 draw count), so that entry is a dash as well. The $t$/$\bar t$ split of the staged schemes is positional rather than physical: the accept/reject fills slot~0 -- $t$, the first final-state particle of every production event here -- against a production density traced over the other slot, which is close to unpolarised and so accepts readily, and slot~1 ($\bar t$) against the fully conditioned parent. The two \texttt{PA} blocks differ only in \texttt{density\_keep\_jacobian}: with the default \texttt{True} the production-reshuffling phase-space jacobian is folded into the accept/reject weight, while ``no jac.'' (\texttt{False}) leaves it out and applies the reshuffle as a post-acceptance kinematic dressing instead. The two therefore unweight against different weights and are expected to have different efficiencies; the option is \texttt{PA}-only and is ignored by \texttt{madspin} (which always carries that jacobian) and by \texttt{onshell} (which never reshuffles). $N^P_{\mathrm{off}}$ is the number of \emph{offshell production} density matrices the unweighting loop evaluates, and it is in the table because it is the expensive object of a MadSpin run: an offshell mass set -- a production reshuffle and then this density -- takes about 90\,$\mu$s against about 8\,$\mu$s for a \texttt{PA} one, which reshuffles too but evaluates no density, so nearly the whole difference is the density itself. $\epsilon_m$ counts mass sets and so prices the two the same. $N^P_{\mathrm{off}}$ is exactly zero under \texttt{PA} and \texttt{onshell}: those evaluate their production density at the \emph{onshell} momenta, once per production event and cached on it, so the whole \texttt{PA}/\texttt{onshell} half of the table pays a flat 100\,000 (one per production event) whatever the scheme, and none of it offshell. Under \texttt{madspin} it is one per \texttt{joint} trial, and one per mass set \emph{drawn} in the staged schemes -- drawn and not accepted, since the density is what the mass-set weight is built from. It is one column and not two because the per-event bound is reached only under \texttt{PA}, where this count is zero, so it cannot move; that is asserted when the table is built. It is also the column that disagrees with $N_{\mathrm{dec}}$ about which scheme is cheapest, which is the reason to print both. In the \texttt{madspin} block \texttt{sequential\_global\_retry} draws \emph{fewer} decays than \texttt{joint} (757\,657 against 858\,196, 12\,\% fewer) and yet evaluates 3.4 times as many production densities (1\,449\,758 against 429\,098), because a rejected decay there throws the mass set away and every new mass set is a fresh production reshuffle and a fresh density. On the wall clock the production densities win and $N_{\mathrm{dec}}$ is the misleading one: on the \emph{before} campaign that row is the slowest of the twelve, 110\,s against 91\,s for \texttt{madspin}/\texttt{joint} and 96\,s for \texttt{madspin}/\texttt{sequential} (8 cores). The after campaign's timings are not usable for that check and no timing here is taken from them: its six \texttt{madspin} and \texttt{onshell} rows reproduce the before campaign's counters exactly, to the last unit of every column, and still took 1.4--2.2 times as long, so they measure the machine's load and not the code. The bound is what changed: the sequential mass stage used to test its weight against a single run-wide number extrapolated from the probe (mean $+\,$\texttt{nb\_sigma}$\,\times\,$sd over the first \texttt{Nevents\_for\_max\_weight} events, times 1.10) and now tests it against an exact per-production-event maximum. It is reached only for \texttt{spinmode = PA} and only in the two up-front schemes, so exactly the rows \texttt{PA\_sequential}, \texttt{PA\_sequential\_global\_retry}, \texttt{PAnojac\_sequential}, \texttt{PAnojac\_sequential\_global\_retry} move; \texttt{madspin} keeps the global bound by decision (its mass weight carries a factor with no cheap maximum) and \texttt{onshell} has no mass stage to bound, and their halves are identical here rather than merely close. The statistical uncertainties quoted here use a geometric-count model: a redraw-until-accepted loop makes a geometric number of trials per written event, mean $\epsilon$ and variance $\epsilon(\epsilon-1)$, so the mean over the $N$ written events has $\sigma = \sqrt{\epsilon(\epsilon-1)/N}$. On that model the largest entry in the table carries $\sigma = 0.044$ ($\epsilon_m$ of \texttt{PA\_sequential\_global\_retry}, $\epsilon_m$ of \texttt{madspin\_sequential\_global\_retry}) and every other is smaller, so the second decimal is the last digit worth reading. It is a floor rather than a bound: the acceptance probability varies from event to event, which adds $2\,\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon_e)$ to the variance, and \texttt{sequential\_global\_retry} couples its stages so its mass count is not a per-stage geometric at all. Because the accepted density is $q_e(m)\,\min(1, w/C)$, any $C \ge \max w$ cancels out of it: the bound sets the cost and not the sample, and the lineshape measurement in \texttt{MadSpin/validation/mt\_lineshape} is the check that it does.}}
