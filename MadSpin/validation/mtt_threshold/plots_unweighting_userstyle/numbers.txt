-- per-bin SHAPE ratio to PA_joint, PA -- THIS IS THE FIGURE'S RATIO PANE --
   UNCLIPPED.  The pane clips to +-20 %; every value outside that
   is drawn on the boundary as a triangle and its real value is
   here.

   Denominator: PA_joint, the row's joint cell -- a SIBLING of every
   other column, not the truth.  Both sides are divided by their
   own total sigma first, so this is a shape ratio and the
   overweight rate carry is divided out of it.

   THREE ERRORS PER BIN, AND THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS.

   "own"      -- what the FIGURE now draws.  Each curve's own
                 statistical error, and the band around 1 is
                 PA_joint's own (the "band" column).  Delta method:
                     var(R) = sum_i (n_i - R d_i)^2 / D^2,
                 the linearised error of a self-normalised
                 histogram, in which the bin is a SUBSET of the
                 sigma it is divided by.  It returns zero when the
                 bin is proportional to the normalisation, which a
                 plain sqrt(sum w^2) does not; the naive form is
                 0.24 % too large on these bins.
                 DO NOT add the band and a bar in quadrature.  That
                 discards the correlation below and OVERESTIMATES
                 the error on the difference.

   "paired"   -- the CORRECT error on the difference between a
                 scheme and PA_joint, and the one to quote for any
                 significance.  The cells decay the SAME production
                 events in the same order (max |Delta sqrt(shat)| =
                 0 GeV over 1 000 000 pairs), so the production
                 fluctuation is common to numerator and denominator
                 and cancels.  The covariance is MEASURED on these
                 very bins by "run_mtt_unweighting.py --stage
                 paired-bins", which counts how many production
                 events land in the SAME bin under both cells; a
                 per-window coincidence count cannot answer this,
                 because an event can be in one window under both
                 schemes and in a different bin under each.

   "unpaired" -- the paired error with the covariance term dropped:
                 what an independent-samples bar would have been.
                 The ratio of it to "paired" is what the shared
                 production sample bought, per bin.  It is also
                 what band-and-bar-in-quadrature amounts to, which
                 is why that reading is wrong.

   PA: every cell holds 1 000 000 events and pairs with PA_joint over all of them.

   other column, not the truth.  Both sides are divided by their
   own total sigma first, so this is a shape ratio and the
   overweight rate carry is divided out of it.

   THREE ERRORS PER BIN, AND THEY ANSWER DIFFERENT QUESTIONS.

   "own"      -- what the FIGURE now draws.  Each curve's own
                 statistical error, and the band around 1 is
                 ms_joint's own (the "band" column).  Delta method:
                     var(R) = sum_i (n_i - R d_i)^2 / D^2,
                 the linearised error of a self-normalised
                 histogram, in which the bin is a SUBSET of the
                 sigma it is divided by.  It returns zero when the
                 bin is proportional to the normalisation, which a
                 plain sqrt(sum w^2) does not; the naive form is
                 0.23 % too large on these bins.
                 DO NOT add the band and a bar in quadrature.  That
                 discards the correlation below and OVERESTIMATES
                 the error on the difference.

   "paired"   -- the CORRECT error on the difference between a
                 scheme and ms_joint, and the one to quote for any
                 significance.  The cells decay the SAME production
                 events in the same order (max |Delta sqrt(shat)| =
                 0 GeV over 500 000 pairs), so the production
                 fluctuation is common to numerator and denominator
                 and cancels.  The covariance is MEASURED on these
                 very bins by "run_mtt_unweighting.py --stage
                 paired-bins", which counts how many production
                 events land in the SAME bin under both cells; a
                 per-window coincidence count cannot answer this,
                 because an event can be in one window under both
                 schemes and in a different bin under each.

   "unpaired" -- the paired error with the covariance term dropped:
                 what an independent-samples bar would have been.
                 The ratio of it to "paired" is what the shared
                 production sample bought, per bin.  It is also
                 what band-and-bar-in-quadrature amounts to, which
                 is why that reading is wrong.

   madspin: PARTLY paired.  ms_joint is decayed from 1 000 000 production events; 
   the other cells hold 500 000, a FRONT TRUNCATION of the same file, so the
   coincidences exist over that prefix only.  The denominator keeps all
   1 000 000 of its events -- only its shared half is credited as correlated,
   and its unshared half enters as an independent error, which is why the
   pairing gain is smaller in this row than in PA.

-- 1. the binning, and why it is not the m_tt one --------------------
   m_tt used zone_edges(): built for a threshold at 2 m_t in a spectrum
   running to 520 GeV.  This is a Breit-Wigner and needs a different
   grid.
     pole        m_t = 173.0000 GeV
     width       Gamma_t = 1.4915 GeV
     range       150.6275 .. 195.3725 GeV = m_t +- 15 Gamma_t, EXACTLY the
                 run's Breit-Wigner cut (bwcutoff = 15 Gamma_t).  The
                 harvest grid is m_t +- 16 Gamma_t and holds nothing
                 outside the cut -- checked, per cell, below.
     zones       |m - m_t| / Gamma_t     bin width
                  0.0 .. 2.0             0.29830 GeV = 1/5 Gamma_t
                  2.0 .. 5.0             0.49717 GeV = 1/3 Gamma_t
                  5.0 .. 9.0             0.99433 GeV = 2/3 Gamma_t
                  9.0 .. 15.0            1.49150 GeV = 1 Gamma_t
     bins        62 in total, every edge on a harvested fine-bin edge
                 (the harvest grid is exactly Gamma_t/75, so the
                 rebinning is exact and is asserted, not rounded).

   Gamma_t/5 in the core is five bins across the FWHM, which is what
   "resolve the lineshape" means for a Breit-Wigner.  The widening
   outwards keeps the per-bin precision inside 0.19-2.7 % instead of
   the 0.16-4.8 % a uniform grid of the same bin count gives, and it
   costs nothing: the Fisher error on a rigid shift of the peak is
   0.00077 GeV on this grid and 0.00077 GeV on a 150-bin uniform
   Gamma_t/5 grid.  Tail bins carry no information about where the peak
   is, so widening them is free.

-- 2. the pairing: what it buys HERE, measured -----------------------
   On m_tt the cells share their production events and fluctuate
   together, so the paired error on a difference is 1.10-1.16 times
   SMALLER than band-and-bar in quadrature, and numbers.txt says
   loudly that the quadrature reading is wrong there.

   On the virtuality it is different, and the difference is measured,
   not argued.  Each scheme re-draws its own masses; the shared
   production kinematics barely constrains m(W b) at all.  The stored
   per-event paired moments say so directly -- compare the PAIRED error
   on <m_top(a)> - <m_top(b)> with the unpaired quadrature of the two
   cells at the same n:

   PA_seq vs PA_globalretry              0.0032007    0.0032034    0.99916
   PA_seq vs PA_joint                    0.0032004    0.0032017    0.99960

   Pairing buys between 0.01 % and 0.4 % on this observable.  The
   equivalent per-event correlation of the two cells' virtualities is
   rho = 0.001-0.008, against rho ~ 0.13 for m_tt bin membership.

   CONSEQUENCE.  The per-bin coincidence counts that "--stage
   paired-bins" produced for m_tt were never produced for the
   virtuality, and cannot be now: they need the decayed LHE files.  The
   error on a difference below is therefore the QUADRATURE of the two
   curves' own errors -- and on this observable that IS the paired
   answer, to better than half a per cent, by the table above.  This is
   a property of m(W b), not a general licence: the same construction
   on m_tt is 10-16 % too large and must not be used there.

-- 6. two things the errors above do NOT know, both measured ---------
==============================================================================

   (i) THE TWO TOPS OF AN EVENT ARE NOT INDEPENDENT, and every error
       here treats them as if they were (n = 2 N).  The evidence is the
       chi2 of the PA row, which is a genuine null and comes out BELOW
       1 on every binning tried (0.56-0.88 over grids from 10 to 150
       bins).  An event's two virtualities compete for the same shat,
       so they are anti-correlated, and a histogram of 2 N tops
       fluctuates LESS than 2 N independent draws would.

       Which way this cuts: the quoted per-bin errors are CONSERVATIVE,
       so the agreement below looks BETTER than it is.  If the true
       per-bin error is sqrt(0.7) = 0.84 of the quoted one, then every
       departure is 1.19 times more significant than the figure's bars
       suggest, and sigma(delta) is 0.84 times the value in section 3.
       The same assumption is in the <m(W b)> moment errors already on
       record (rms/sqrt(2N)), so it is inherited, not introduced here.
       Settling it needs the per-event pair of virtualities, which the
       harvest did not keep and the LHE files can no longer supply.
       A future harvest should store the per-event mean of the two.

   (ii) ms_joint IS THE madspin ROW'S DENOMINATOR AND IT CARRIES A
       RATE ARTEFACT.  The overweight safety net wrote 754 of its 1M
       events with weight above 1 (largest factor 83, +0.296 % of
       sigma).  On the virtuality that is not a small effect:
