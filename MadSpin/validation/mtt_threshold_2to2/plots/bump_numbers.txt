THE QUESTION.  The figure draws (1/sigma) dsigma/dm_tt.  Every spinmode
is EXACTLY zero below 2 m_t and the shape ratio to the truth rises to
about 1.12 just above it.  Is that bump a normalisation artefact of the
self-normalisation, and does it compensate the empty region below?

-- 1. the two normalisations, and why the answer differs between them --

   sub-threshold rate the modes miss (truth, m_tt < 2 m_t):
      0.9249 +- 0.0099 pb   = 0.17456% +- 0.00187% of the truth total (529.8240 pb)

   SELF-NORMALISED (what the figure draws).  Each curve is divided by
   its own total sigma, so each integrates to 1 over the full m_tt
   range.  A mode with zero sub-threshold rate therefore has its
   above-threshold curve scaled up by 1/(1 - f_below) relative to the
   truth SHAPE:
      1/(1 - 0.0017456) - 1 = +0.17487%   -- a FLAT rescaling, every bin
   The visible bump is nothing like that size.  Largest drawn shape
   ratio above threshold, and the flat artefact for comparison:
      madspin     max shape ratio 1.122 +- 0.030 in 349-350 GeV  =  +12.2%, i.e. 70 x the flat artefact
      PA          max shape ratio 1.122 +- 0.030 in 349-350 GeV  =  +12.2%, i.e. 70 x the flat artefact
      onshell     max shape ratio 1.122 +- 0.030 in 349-350 GeV  =  +12.2%, i.e. 70 x the flat artefact
      madspin_v1  max shape ratio 1.122 +- 0.030 in 349-350 GeV  =  +12.2%, i.e. 70 x the flat artefact
   => the bump is a REDISTRIBUTION of rate, not the self-normalisation.

   ABSOLUTE pb.  The two sides do NOT have the same total: the truth
   loses 4.24% to the Breit-Wigner truncation (RESULTS.md section 3),
   which is a global rate loss and has nothing to do with the threshold.
      madspin     sigma 553.2907 pb vs truth 529.8240 pb;  above 2 m_t the mode exceeds the truth by +24.392 pb = 26.4 x the 0.9249 pb it misses below
      PA          sigma 553.3175 pb vs truth 529.8240 pb;  above 2 m_t the mode exceeds the truth by +24.418 pb = 26.4 x the 0.9249 pb it misses below
      onshell     sigma 553.2790 pb vs truth 529.8240 pb;  above 2 m_t the mode exceeds the truth by +24.380 pb = 26.4 x the 0.9249 pb it misses below
      madspin_v1  sigma 553.2790 pb vs truth 529.8240 pb;  above 2 m_t the mode exceeds the truth by +24.380 pb = 26.4 x the 0.9249 pb it misses below
   => in pb the bump compensates NOTHING.  The rate question can only
      be asked after the totals are matched, which is the third
      normalisation below.

   the parent study measures truth/mode = 0.9576 on the totals against

   that same 0.6% is visible above as the +0.105% +- 0.041% the
   cumulative picks up over [380, 420).  Renormalising each mode on the
   380-420 GeV anchor instead of its total moves the answer to:

      madspin     anchor-normalised  +0.13454% +- 0.02683%   = 0.77 x f_below   (total-normalised: 1.00 x)

      onshell     anchor-normalised  +0.13244% +- 0.02683%   = 0.76 x f_below   (total-normalised: 0.99 x)
      madspin_v1  anchor-normalised  +0.13244% +- 0.02683%   = 0.76 x f_below   (total-normalised: 0.99 x)

   So the excess is 0.75 to 1.0 times the sub-threshold rate depending
   on which of the two defensible normalisations is used, against a
   statistical error of 0.15.  The normalisation choice, not the
   statistics, is what limits how sharply the compensation can be
   stated -- and it is a broad-band effect that the parent study
   already records, not anything to do with the threshold.

   Above 370 GeV nothing further accumulates:
      onshell     [370, 520) GeV  +0.07566% +- 0.05460%   (1.4 sigma from zero) -- flat

   CAVEAT, stated because it is what makes the self-normalised answer
   partly an identity.  Over the FULL m_tt range the self-normalised
   excess above threshold is forced to equal f_below exactly, because
   both curves integrate to 1 and the mode has nothing below.  The
   MEASURED content is not that the total comes out right -- it is
   WHERE it comes out: all of it inside 2 m_t + 24 GeV, over a window
   holding 6.27% of the cross section, and flat above.
   The histogram itself stops at 520 GeV and holds 60.3% of sigma, so
   the identity is not available to the numbers above as a shortcut.

   of it within ~1 GeV of 2 m_t; onshell and madspin_v1 took none and

      to compare: the excess being measured is 0.17230% +- 0.02683%
   => the overweights move the answer by at most 24% of its own
      statistical error, so they do NOT contaminate it.  The clean
      modes are onshell and madspin_v1 (zero overweights), and every
      headline number above is quoted from onshell for that reason.
   NB the overweights ARE the whole of the visible spread between the
   four modes in the 346-347 GeV bin (shape ratios 0.812 / 0.842 /
   0.900), which is why that bin is not used to define anything here,
   and why the integration window starts at 2 m_t and not inside it.

-- 5. the mechanism, tested and not asserted -------------------------

   HYPOTHESIS.  The truth's m_tt is the on-shell production spectrum
   smeared by the two top virtualities: at virtualities m1, m2 the pair
   threshold is m1 + m2, not 2 m_t.  The production spectrum is zero
   below 2 m_t and rises like sqrt(M - 2 m_t) above it -- CONCAVE -- so
   smearing it (i) fills the region below threshold, (ii) RAISES the
   first GeV above it, where the sharp spectrum is still near zero, and
   (iii) LOWERS the concave rise that follows.  MadSpin's m_tt is the
   unsmeared spectrum (m_tt = sqrt(shat) at this multiplicity, held
   fixed by the reshuffle), so mode/truth < 1 in the first bin and > 1
   over the following ~10 GeV.  That is exactly the measured pattern.

   TEST.  Smear the committed production histogram with the same
   Breit-Wigner the truth used -- fixed-width relativistic, truncated at
   15 Gamma_t, times the decay numerator m Gamma(m)/(m_t Gamma_t) -- and
   compare with the truth.  Two independent approximations:
      A  threshold shift : M -> M + (m1 + m2 - 2 m_t)
      B  velocity factor: dsigma_prod/dM / beta_0(M) fitted and
         extrapolated below 2 m_t, times the exact beta(M; m1, m2)
   Neither is tuned to the truth.  Model B carries no absolute
   normalisation of its own and is anchored on the production sample
   over 380-420 GeV.

   VERDICT.  The smeared production sample reproduces the truth's
   turn-on to a few percent bin by bin where MadSpin is off by 19%,
   and recovers most of the sub-threshold rate where MadSpin has none.
   The residual of the model is a roughly uniform ~1% normalisation
   offset against the truth over the whole drawn window, not a failure
   of the turn-on shape -- so the smearing accounts for the bump and
   slightly overshoots it.  The model is a leading-order description
   (the matrix element is taken to depend on the virtualities only
   through the phase space) and is NOT a substitute for the truth.

-- 6. what this does not settle -------------------------------------

   * The model's ~1% normalisation offset is not resolved into its
     parts.  Candidates: the 4.24% Breit-Wigner truncation is not
     perfectly flat in m_tt (the parent study measures truth/mode as
     0.9576 on the total against 0.9517 +- 0.0025 on the 380-420 GeV
     anchor, a 0.6% difference), and the model's neglect of the
     virtuality dependence of the matrix element.  Separating them
     needs a truth run at a second bwcutoff, which does not exist.
   * Only bwcutoff = 15 was run, so how far below threshold the truth
     reaches -- and hence the size of everything measured here -- is a
     parameter, not a prediction.
   * Single- and non-resonant W b W b~ diagrams are absent from BOTH
     sides (the truth is an MG5 decay chain), so nothing here bounds
     their contribution near threshold.
   * One seed per mode.  The overweight numbers of section 4 are one
     draw; the rest does not depend on them.
