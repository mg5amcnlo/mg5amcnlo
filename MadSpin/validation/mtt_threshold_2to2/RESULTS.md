# `m_tt` near `2 m_t` for `p p > t t~`: no spinmode reaches below threshold

`p p > t t~` at 13 TeV, LO, `mu_R = mu_F = m_t`, no cuts. Truth:
`p p > t t~, t > w+ b, t~ > w- b~` generated by MG5 (doubly-resonant, both tops
off shell out to `bwcutoff = 15` widths), **5 000 000** events. MadSpin:
**1 000 000** production events, decayed with `t > w+ b` / `t~ > w- b~` and
`BW_cut = 15`, once per spinmode, all four off the same production sample and
the same seed. `m_tt` is the mass of `(W+ b) + (W- b~)` on both sides.

Figure: `plots/mtt_threshold.pdf` (MG7 style),
`plots_userstyle/mtt_threshold.pdf` (user style). Full per-bin table:
`plots/numbers.txt`. Raw histograms: `data/histograms.npz`, provenance
`data/meta.json`.

This is the control experiment for
[`../mtt_threshold`](../mtt_threshold/RESULTS.md), which asks the same question
of `p p > t t~ j`. That study's section 6 says the multiplicity dependence "is
not measured here". This measures it.

---

## 0. The answer

**No spinmode moves `m_tt` at all, and the sub-threshold region is structurally
empty for every one of the four.** Measured event by event on 1 000 000 paired
events per mode:

| spinmode | mean `Delta m_tt` | rms | max abs | max abs `Delta sqrt(shat)` | `m_tt` unchanged | events pushed below `2 m_t` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `madspin` | +2.1e-08 GeV | 9.2e-08 GeV | **3.3e-06 GeV** | **0** | **100 %** | **0 of 1 000 000** |
| `PA` | +2.1e-08 GeV | 9.2e-08 GeV | **2.8e-06 GeV** | **0** | **100 %** | **0 of 1 000 000** |
| `onshell` | +1.2e-10 GeV | 3.1e-07 GeV | **1.6e-05 GeV** | **0** | **100 %** | **0 of 1 000 000** |
| `madspin_v1` | +4.0e-11 GeV | 6.7e-08 GeV | **2.3e-06 GeV** | **0** | **100 %** | **0 of 1 000 000** |

Compare the `t t~ j` numbers, same code, same observable, one extra final-state
parton: `madspin` +0.137 GeV / rms 2.14 GeV / max 44.5 GeV / **1382** below
threshold, `PA` -0.028 / 2.10 / 45.4 / **1825**, `madspin_v1` +0.092 / 2.64 /
140.6 / **458**. Seven orders of magnitude in the largest excursion, and 1382,
1825, 458 against 0, 0, 0.

**The pairing check.** `max |Delta sqrt(shat)| = 0` to the last bit in every
event of every mode. MadSpin writes one decayed event per production event, in
order, and `sqrt(shat)` is RAMBO-invariant, so a zero here is what proves the two
event streams are really aligned -- 1 000 000 / 1 000 000 for every mode, no
malformed events on either side. Without it the `Delta m_tt` column would be a
comparison of unrelated events.

**The residuals are the LHE text precision, not physics.** `max |Delta m_tt|` is
2.3e-06 to 1.6e-05 GeV. The LHE writes momenta as decimal text, so an event whose
momenta were never touched still round-trips with a `m_tt` that differs in the
last digits: in the `t t~ j` study `onshell` -- which provably never touches
them -- measured 2.6e-05 GeV on exactly this floor. Every mode here is at or
below that number. All four are 100 % unchanged at `|Delta m_tt|/m_tt < 1e-6`;
the bit-exact fractions (5.05 %, 5.00 %, 4.51 %, 18.72 %) are a statement about
how many decimal digits the file carries and nothing else.

### Why, in one line each

`Event.reshuffle_production` hands **every** top-level final-state momentum to
RAMBO's `mass_shuffle` at fixed `sqrt(shat)`. With a recoil jet present that
rescales the jet too, the `t t~` four-momentum is not preserved, and `m_tt`
moves. Here the tops **are** the final state, so `m_tt = sqrt(shat)` identically
and the reshuffle is holding the observable fixed by construction, whatever
virtualities were drawn.

* `madspin` -- draws the virtuality from the off-shell density matrix, then
  reshuffles. The reshuffle cannot move `m_tt`.
* `PA` -- draws it from a fixed-width Breit-Wigner, then reshuffles. Same.
* `onshell` -- draws no virtuality and `_density_do_reshuffle` is False; the
  momenta are never touched at all.
* `madspin_v1` -- never calls `reshuffle_production`. It regenerates the
  phase-space point from the decay-chain topology holding `shat` and the
  production tree's invariants fixed (`generate_momenta_conf` / `keep_inv` in
  `MadSpin/src/driver.f`). With two final-state particles the `t t~` invariant
  **is** `shat`, so what it holds fixed is the observable.

Four different code paths, one kinematic reason. In the `t t~ j` study these four
gave four different answers, and `madspin_v1` in particular got below threshold
by a mechanism the other three do not use; the multiplicity removes the
distinction entirely.

### The strongest form of the claim

Not just "empty below threshold" -- **each mode's `m_tt` histogram is identical
to the undecayed production sample's, bin for bin, on the raw 0.25 GeV grid**:

```
madspin     identical: True   (603908 vs 603908 entries in range, max |difference| = 0)
PA          identical: True   (603908 vs 603908 entries in range, max |difference| = 0)
onshell     identical: True   (603908 vs 603908 entries in range, max |difference| = 0)
madspin_v1  identical: True   (603908 vs 603908 entries in range, max |difference| = 0)
```

The production sample is on-shell `t t~` and has **0 of 1 000 000** events below
`2 m_t`. Every mode inherits that exactly. In the `t t~ j` study only `onshell`
was in this position.

## 1. Below threshold, as a count

The truth reaches where no spinmode does. `sigma(m_tt < 2 m_t)` over the full
histogram range (290 GeV up; **0** events fell below 290 GeV on either side, so
the range is not clipping anything):

| | `sigma(m_tt < 2 m_t)` [pb] | events | as a fraction of its own total |
|---|---|---|---|
| truth | 0.9249 +- 0.0099 | **8728 of 5 000 000** | 0.175 % |
| `madspin` | **0 exactly** | **0 of 1 000 000** | 0 |
| `PA` | **0 exactly** | **0 of 1 000 000** | 0 |
| `onshell` | **0 exactly** | **0 of 1 000 000** | 0 |
| `madspin_v1` | **0 exactly** | **0 of 1 000 000** | 0 |

Every one of those zeros is **structural**, not a sample-size accident, and the
justification is the same for all four: `m_tt` comes back unchanged event by
event (section 0), and the sample it comes from has no support there. It would
be 0 at any `N`.

So **all four modes miss 100 % of the sub-threshold cross section, which is
0.175 % of the total.** In the `t t~ j` study that sentence was true of `onshell`
alone; `madspin` got the integrated sub-threshold rate to within 13 %, `PA` to
within 15 % and `madspin_v1` to within 71 %. None of that survives the removal of
the jet, because none of it was a property of the virtuality model -- it was the
recoil.

### The drawing convention, applied to all four

The `t t~ j` figure draws an exact structural zero as an **open circle on the
lower boundary of the clipped ratio pane, with no arrow**, so it stays distinct
from a measured point that ran off the pane (arrow) and from an empty bin that
only reflects `N` (gap). Here **all four modes** earn the circle, in all nine
sub-threshold plot bins.

The circles coincide -- that is the result -- so they are drawn **concentric**,
smallest on top. Nudging them apart horizontally is not available: the turn-on is
binned at 1 GeV, which is about 4 pt of axis, and four 5 pt markers do not fit
side by side in it. `plots/numbers.txt` lists which modes carry circles and in
how many bins, so the convention is auditable and not just visible.

**No point leaves the clipped pane in either style.** Zero arrows are drawn, and
that is itself a result: in the `t t~ j` figure fourteen shape ratios were off
scale.

## 2. Above threshold: four curves, one sample, and the overweights

Because every mode's event **counts** are identical to the production sample's,
the only thing that can separate the drawn densities is the event **weights** --
and a MadSpin sample has unit weights except where the overweight safety net
carried a trial weight that exceeded its accept/reject bound. Measured, in units
of one nominal event (553.279 pb):

| mode | excess weight [events] | of which within 1 GeV of `2 m_t` | shift in `sigma` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `madspin` (joint) | 21.11 | **21.11 (100 %)** | +0.0021 % |
| `PA` (sequential) | 69.53 | **62.13 (89 %)** | +0.0070 % |
| `onshell` (sequential) | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| `madspin_v1` (legacy) | 0 | 0 | 0 |

That is the **whole** of the visible spread between the curves. In the first bin
above threshold, 346-347 GeV, the shape ratios to truth are `madspin`
0.842 +- 0.038, `PA` 0.900 +- 0.042, `onshell` 0.812 +- 0.036 and `madspin_v1`
0.812 +- 0.036 -- and `onshell` and `madspin_v1` are equal to the last digit
because they are the same sample, weight for weight. Everything above about
348 GeV is four curves on top of each other.

Two things follow.

* **The spread near threshold is an accept/reject artefact, not a spinmode
  difference.** It is the accept/reject bound being hardest to satisfy exactly
  where the phase space is tightest: at `m_tt` barely above `2 m_t` there is
  almost nothing left for two off-shell tops. `PA` under `sequential` took 186
  overweights (largest factor 5.14) and `madspin` under `joint` took 61 (largest
  factor 3.61). Both were **carried**, not clipped, so the excess is in the
  central values and in the errors alike; the logs in `data/logs/` say so
  per run.
* **The `madspin`-vs-`PA` question this framework was built to ask has no
  content at this multiplicity.** In the `t t~ j` study `PA` put 32 % more events
  below threshold than `madspin` and the two bracketed the truth from opposite
  sides. Here they produce the same events with the same `m_tt`, and the
  virtuality they drew is invisible in this observable. It is not invisible in
  *every* observable -- the tops really are off shell, and `m_t` reconstructed
  from `W b` still carries the lineshape (that is
  `MadSpin/validation/mt_lineshape/`'s question, not this one) -- but `m_tt`
  cannot see it.

### Where the shape agrees

Shape normalisation (each side over its own total `sigma`), scanned downwards
from 420 GeV over the plot binning:

| tolerance | `madspin` | `PA` | `onshell` | `madspin_v1` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 %, strict | `m_tt >= 354 GeV` (1.041 +- 0.023) | `m_tt >= 354 GeV` (1.040 +- 0.023) | `m_tt >= 354 GeV` (1.041 +- 0.023) | `m_tt >= 354 GeV` (1.041 +- 0.023) |
| 10 %, strict | `m_tt >= 350 GeV` (1.095 +- 0.028) | `m_tt >= 350 GeV` (1.095 +- 0.028) | `m_tt >= 350 GeV` (1.095 +- 0.028) | `m_tt >= 350 GeV` (1.095 +- 0.028) |

**Shape agreement to 5 % returns at `2 m_t + 8 GeV` for all four modes, and to
10 % at `2 m_t + 4 GeV`.** Rescaling instead by each mode's local 380-420 GeV
anchor (0.9517 +- 0.0025 for all four) gives the same edge in all eight cells,
which is the evidence that the rate offset of section 3 is flat and not a shape.

In the `t t~ j` study the four modes came back at `2 m_t + 7`, `+10`, `+14` and
`+14 GeV` respectively and the ordering was the physics: the mode that samples
the virtuality from the matrix element recovered first. **Here the four numbers
are the same number.** What is left between `2 m_t` and `2 m_t + 8 GeV` is not a
spinmode difference at all: it is the difference between an on-shell `t t~`
turn-on and an off-shell one, identical for every mode, and it is what an
off-shell truth buys that MadSpin structurally cannot at this multiplicity.

## 3. The raw rates, recorded for the change in review

The figure divides these out. They are on record because a change now in review
makes MadSpin's reported cross section carry the Breit-Wigner truncation, and
**this branch does not contain it** -- so these are the numbers that change.

| sample | `sigma` [pb] | `truth / sample` |
|---|---|---|
| truth | **529.8240** | -- |
| `madspin` | 553.2907 | **0.95759** |
| `PA` | 553.3175 | **0.95754** |
| `onshell` | 553.2790 | **0.95761** |
| `madspin_v1` | 553.2790 | **0.95761** |
| production (undecayed) | 553.2790 | -- |

Banner cross sections: truth 529.8350 pb, everything MadSpin 553.2790 pb. The
three `truth/mode` values differ only through the overweight excess of
section 2 (0.007 % at most), so there is **one** number to explain: **4.24 %**.

It is not statistics. MadEvent quotes 529.8 +- 0.0505 pb for one truth run and
553.3 +- 0.0468 pb for the production, i.e. 0.010 % and 0.008 %, and the five
truth runs (529.835, 529.843, 529.758, 529.874, 529.810 pb) spread by 0.022 %.
It is not a branching-ratio mismatch either: the model's own LO
`Gamma(t -> W b)` at `m_t = 173`, `m_b = 4.7`, derived `MW = 80.4185` is
1.49148 GeV against the param card's `WT = 1.49150 GeV`, an implied BR of
0.99998.

It is the truncation. MG5's decay-chain truth rejects every phase-space point
with `|m - m_t| >= 15 Gamma_t` (`myamp.f`, the `gForceBW = 1` branch: the point
is *rejected*, so the loss is in the integrated cross section), while MadSpin's
`sigma_production * BR` takes no such loss. The four NWA-normalised evaluations
of that integral depend only on the model parameters and on `bwcutoff`, both
unchanged from the `t t~ j` study, so they come out at the same numbers:

| per-resonance kept fraction | per res | pair | residual vs measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-relativistic BW, flat numerator `[1-2*atan(30)/pi]` | 0.97879 | 0.95802 | |
| fixed-width relativistic BW, flat numerator | 0.97870 | 0.95785 | **-0.03 %** |
| `+` the decay numerator `m Gamma(m)/(m_t Gamma_t)` | 0.98106 | 0.96249 | -0.51 % |
| `+` `d ln sigma_prod/dm_t = -1.5 %/GeV` per top (an **input**) | 0.97824 | 0.95695 | +0.06 % |
| **measured `truth/PA`** | | **0.95754** | |

The truncation removes 4.2 % where the whole measured difference is 4.2 %, and
the residual is **-0.5 % to +0.1 %** depending on which numerator variant it is
measured against. As in the `t t~ j` study that is a range, not a measurement:
`d ln sigma_prod / d m_t` is an input this study never varied, and pinning it
down needs a truth run at a second `bwcutoff`, which was not done.

**Do not read anything into the fact that this residual is smaller than the
`t t~ j` study's +0.4 % to +1.0 %.** These are different production processes
with different `m_t` slopes, so the two residuals are not required to agree, and
neither one is a measurement of the other.

## 4. Statistics, and what the errors will and will not support

* truth 5 x 1 000 000 events (MG5 caps one `generate_events` at 1M --
  `madevent_interface.check_nb_events` -- so this is five independent runs with
  consecutive seeds 4242-4246, summed). 8728 of them below `2 m_t`. This matches
  the `t t~ j` study's statistics exactly; nothing was reduced.
* MadSpin 1 000 000 production events per spinmode, one shared production
  sample, one seed (42). **0** below `2 m_t` in each.
* **The sub-threshold claim needs no per-bin statistics at all**, which is the
  one place this study is easier than its sibling. It is not "the bins are
  empty within errors" -- it is "the momenta were measured, event by event, not
  to have changed, and the sample they came from has no support there". The
  count `0 of 1 000 000` is a consequence, not the evidence.
* What the statistics *do* limit is section 2. The deepest **drawn** bin,
  316-326 GeV, holds 32 truth events, and no conclusion is drawn from it; the
  truth ratios quoted above threshold rest on 3500-12000 events per 1 GeV bin
  and carry 0.9-1.7 % errors on the truth side (2-4 % on the ratio), which is what
  the `+8 GeV` and `+4 GeV` agreement edges are quoted against.
* No bias and no phase-space cut was used to concentrate events near threshold:
  the samples are the plain inclusive process. The sub-threshold region is
  0.175 % of the cross section.
* **The accept/reject schemes differ between modes and were not controlled
  for.** `unweighting = auto` sent `madspin` to `joint` and `PA`/`onshell` to
  `sequential` (`_auto_unweighting_mode`'s documented rule for two decaying
  particles); `madspin_v1` ran the legacy path, which never reaches
  `_unweighting_mode` at all. Each mode therefore ran its own shipped default,
  which is the right thing to compare, but the overweight excess of section 2 is
  confounded with the scheme. The `t t~ j` study ran a `madspin`-under-forced-
  `sequential` control; **this study did not** -- see section 5.
* **No replica.** Each spinmode was run once. For the sub-threshold result that
  does not matter -- a different seed cannot make a held-fixed invariant move --
  but the section 2 overweight numbers are one draw and would move seed to seed.

## 5. What this does not cover

* **No accept/reject control was run.** The `t t~ j` study reran `madspin` with
  `unweighting` forced to `sequential` to check that the `madspin`-vs-`PA`
  difference was the spinmode and not the scheme. That control was **not**
  repeated here, deliberately: the difference it exists to disentangle -- the
  sub-threshold rate -- is exactly 0 for both modes, so there is nothing for it
  to resolve. The price is that the section 2 overweight numbers are not
  separated from the scheme: whether `PA` takes 3x `madspin`'s excess because it
  is `PA` or because it ran `sequential` is **not** settled here. Nothing else in
  this document depends on that.
* **Only doubly-resonant diagrams.** The truth is the MG5 decay chain, so
  single-resonant and non-resonant `W+ b W- b~` contributions are absent from
  *both* sides. That isolates MadSpin's kinematic approximation instead of mixing
  it with a class of diagrams the framework never tried to reproduce, but it
  means nothing here bounds the size of those contributions near threshold.
* **On-shell `W`s throughout**, on both sides. `W` virtuality is not probed.
* **One process, one scale, one PDF, LO.** No scale or PDF variation; the fixed
  `mu_R = mu_F = m_t` was chosen so the two sides could not differ through the
  dynamical-scale definition.
* **`m_tt` only.** That the tops' virtuality is invisible in `m_tt` at this
  multiplicity is *not* a statement that the spinmodes agree. They draw different
  virtualities and the decay products carry them; the reconstructed `m_t` from
  `W b` is where that shows, and it is not measured here.
  `MadSpin/validation/mt_lineshape/` is the study for it.
* **Two multiplicities, not a scan.** With `t t~ j` this now covers `2 -> 2` and
  `2 -> 3`. A `2 -> 4` production would move `m_tt` more than `2 -> 3` does, by
  the same argument, and that is not measured. Nor is the dependence on the
  recoil's hardness -- `ptj > 20 GeV` in the sibling study sets the scale of
  `E_j` and so of the effect, and no `ptj` scan was run in either.
* **`bwcutoff` is a parameter of the figure, not a background.** It sets how far
  below `2 m_t` the truth reaches, and only `bwcutoff = 15` was run. The
  sub-threshold cross section the modes miss is proportional to how much of the
  Breit-Wigner tail the truth is allowed to keep.
