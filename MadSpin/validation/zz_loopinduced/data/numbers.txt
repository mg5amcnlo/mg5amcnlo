--- the named coefficients behind the two angular figures ---
  A Z is a WEAK spin analyser.  Its decay to l+ l- carries the analysing
  power eta_l = 2 g_V g_A / (g_V^2 + g_A^2) = 0.21933 for this run
  (MW = 80.4190 from (aEWM1, Gf, MZ), sw2 = 1 - MW^2/MZ^2 = 0.222246), and it
  multiplies ONLY the parity-violating cos(theta) term.  So:

    f_0   = 2 - 5 <cos^2 theta>                      UNDILUTED
            the longitudinal fraction of one z; 1/3 is isotropic.
    f_00  = <(2 - 5 cos^2 th1)(2 - 5 cos^2 th2)>     UNDILUTED
            the joint (both longitudinal) fraction; f_0^2 if the two
            z were independent, so f_00 - f_0^2 is the rank-2
            correlation and the analogue of t t~'s C_nn that survives.
    C_kk  = 4 <cos th1 cos th2> / eta_l^2            DILUTED by eta_l^2
            = <S_k(1) S_k(2)>, each S on its own helicity axis.  The
            calibration is 4 / eta_l^2 = 83.2, not the 9 of t t~: 9 is
            the spin-1/2 algebra with kappa = 1, 4 is the spin-1 one.

--- per-observable agreement over the bins where truth has support ---
  "rate" is the ratio of INTEGRALS over those bins, sum(mode)/sum(truth).
  It is deliberately not an inverse-variance weighted mean of the
  per-bin ratios: the per-bin error is built from the numerator, so a bin
  that fluctuates low gets a smaller error and more weight, and the mean
  comes out biased low -- about 5 % on a 2000-event pilot here, which is
  the size of the effects under study.  The integral ratio has no such
  bias.  "chi2/ndf" is the SHAPE test: the per-bin ratios against the
  best-fit flat line (i.e. against "rate"), so a pure normalisation
  offset does not enter it and only a genuine shape difference does.
