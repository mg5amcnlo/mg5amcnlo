--- IS THE DECAY CARD EXCLUSIVE IN EFFECT? (measured, not assumed) ---
The MadSpin card of every run is
  decay z > e+ e-
  decay z > mu+ mu-
which is exclusive in INTENT.  Whether it is exclusive in EFFECT is a
different question and it decides what these figures mean, so it is
read off the event record -- mark each z's end vertex, follow the chain
of status-44 copies, take the particles born at the final vertex -- for
every event of every file:

   spinmode        events one z->e+e- AND   at least one      other
                          other z->mu+mu-        z->e+e-   channels
   madspin         250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   onshell         250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   PA              250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   madspin_v1      250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   LO              250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0

It is exclusive in effect: every event of every file has one z to e+e-
and the other to mu+mu-, with no other channel present at all.  So
BOTH observables have the full 250 000 events behind them before the
fiducial cuts, and the sparsity that made M(e+ mu+) undrawable on the
earlier decay z > light light samples (312 of 250 000 survivors) is
gone.

WHAT THE CROSS SECTIONS ARE OF.  Because the card is exclusive, the
sigma quoted for each file is
     sigma(p p > z z) x BR(z -> e+e-) x BR(z -> mu+mu-) x 2
(the 2 being the two ways of assigning the channels to the two z), and
NOT an inclusive p p > z z cross section.  The earlier inclusive
samples quoted ~13.1 pb, essentially the production cross section
because decay z > light light has a branching fraction of ~1; these
quote ~0.031 pb, smaller by ~2.35e-03, which is that product of
branching fractions.  The two sets of numbers are NOT comparable as
rates and only the ratios between the modes are.

--- DO THE SAMPLES SHARE PRODUCTION EVENTS? ---
This decides the error bar on every mode-to-mode comparison in this
file, so it is established and not assumed.  If the runs had
decayed one common set of production events, the mode-to-mode ratio
would be CORRELATED, a paired error would be correct, and it would
be much smaller than the quadrature one.  They did not.

spinmode        events     n(w < 0)   sigma_tot pb corr(m_4l) row by row
madspin         250000        14273       0.030874    --  (reference)
onshell         250000        13962       0.032415 -0.00028 +- 0.00203
PA              250000        14099       0.031116 +0.00041 +- 0.00203
madspin_v1      250000        14179       0.030919 -0.00005 +- 0.00203
LO              250000            0       0.023950 -0.00219 +- 0.00203

n(w < 0) is the DECISIVE column and it is permutation invariant.
Neither MadSpin nor Pythia can change the sign of an event weight --
MadSpin multiplies by a positive decay factor and Pythia passes the
LHE weight through -- so the number of negative-weight events is a
property of the PRODUCTION sample alone and cannot depend on the
spinmode or on the order the events are written in.  Two decays of
one production sample would give the same count EXACTLY.  These
differ, so a common production sample is excluded outright, in any
order, and not merely left unproven.

corr(m_4l) corroborates it and is the nearest thing the cached
columns have to the sqrt(shat) the sibling m_tt study paired on.
For a 2 -> 2 production event the four-lepton invariant mass IS the
production m(ZZ), so two decays of one production event would agree
in it to the dressing; row by row it is consistent with ZERO on
~243 000 common rows, where pairing would give ~1.

The .npz carries no production-level column at all -- no
sqrt(shat), no event number, no production kinematics -- so the
literal "max |Delta sqrt(shat)| = 0" test of the m_tt study cannot
be run on this cache.  It does not need to be: the negative-weight
count is a stronger test in the direction that matters, because it
is permutation invariant, and it FAILS.

CONSEQUENCE: every between-mode error in this file and on the
figures is the PLAIN quadrature sum, and that is CORRECT here and
not merely conservative.  A paired bar would be wrong, not just
optimistic.  (Within a single sample the covariance is kept and
must be -- see "integrated polarisation ratios" and
pol_analysis.shape_density.)

--- THE SHAPE RATIO: what the variant-B pane draws ---
   (1/sigma dsigma/dX)_Y / (1/sigma dsigma/dX)_madspin
for every Y in one pane -- Y = onshell, Y = PA and, on this variant
alone, Y = madspin_v1.  Each mode is divided by
its OWN cross section before the division, so the RATE difference
between the modes divides out and what is left is the SHAPE.  That is
the pane this data wants: onshell differs from madspin in rate (+4.99 %
inclusive) and much less in shape, PA agrees in rate (1.4 sigma) and
differs in shape.  A ratio still carrying the normalisation shows the
first as a large flat offset and buries the second underneath it.

WHICH SIGMA NORMALISES EACH CURVE.  Two candidates, and they are not
the same number:
   selected  the whole selected fiducial cross section, every event
             passing the lepton cuts, INCLUDING those whose observable
             falls outside the drawn range;
   inrange   the integral of the drawn histogram, in-range events only.

THE FIGURES USE: inrange
   -- the integral of the drawn histogram (in-range events only)
because it makes the pane an exact statement about what is on the
canvas: the cross-section-weighted mean of the drawn ratio over the
drawn bins is then 1 by construction, so every visible departure from 1
is paid for by another visible bin.  With "selected" the out-of-range
rate -- which is mode dependent -- slides the whole pane by an amount
whose cause is off the canvas.  Both are tabulated below; the choice is
made for that reason and not because it changes an answer.

THE ERROR ON THIS PANE, and it is two different things multiplied
together.  WITHIN one sample the bin content and the sigma that
normalises it are sums over the SAME events -- the bin is a subset of
the normalisation -- so they are correlated and the delta-method error
of pol_analysis.ratio is used, exactly as for the polarisation ratios.
A plain sqrt(sum w^2) would overstate the bar by 1/sqrt(1 - 2 p) for a
bin holding a fraction p of the rate: 8 % on a twelfth of the Delta phi
rate, which would understate the chi2 by 14 %.  BETWEEN samples there
is NO correlation to keep -- see "DO THE SAMPLES SHARE PRODUCTION
EVENTS?" above -- so the two relative errors add in quadrature.  One
degree of freedom is removed from the chi2 because each curve was
divided by its own normalisation, which is exactly one constraint.

THE K-FACTOR FIGURE: NLO/LO, PER POLARISATION COMPONENT
==========================================================================
A FIFTH sample: run_12_decayed_1, the same p p > z z [QCD] process, the
same run card, the same 250 000 events, the same exclusive decay card,
the same spinmode madspin and the same Pythia8 shower as the reference
-- and <run_settings> order = LO where every other run in this study
says order = NLO.  It carries the four ms_pol_* weights (33 names on
its N line, the reference's list exactly), which is what makes a
per-polarisation K-factor possible at all.

ONE MADSPIN CARD LINE DIFFERS BEYOND THE ORDER and it is a polarisation
setting, so it is checked rather than waved through.  run_06 sets
"set frame_id 24"; run_12 leaves it commented out and takes the default
6.  frame_id is the bitmask sum(2**n for n in me_frame), so 24 = legs
3+4 (the two z, i.e. the ZZ rest frame) and 6 = legs 1+2 (the initial
partons).  Those are the same frame exactly when the event has no other
final-state leg, and the LO sample's LHE is 2 -> 2 in every event
(NUP = 4 throughout) while the NLO one carries a fifth leg in about a
fifth of its events.  So both samples quantise the z polarisations
along the ZZ-rest-frame axis and the components being divided are the
same components.  Had the LO run been a real-emission sample this
K-factor would have been ill defined.

NORMALISATION.  THIS FIGURE IS ABSOLUTE AND VARIANT B IS NOT, and that
is deliberate.  A K-factor is a ratio of RATES, so nothing may be
divided out before it is taken: panels 1-5 are dsigma/dx in pb per unit
of the observable and panel 6 is their ratio.  Variant B divides every
curve by its own sigma because it asks about SHAPE; at these cuts the
rate moves 29 % and the shape a few percent, so an unnormalised
variant-B pane would be four curves sitting near 0.78 and nothing else
would be readable.  The two figures answer two different questions.

ERRORS, PER CURVE.
  panels 1-5, both curves: plain MC, sqrt(sum w^2) per bin.  Each is a
    single weighted sum and not a ratio, so there is no covariance for
    a delta-method bar to keep.
  panel 6, all five curves: the two relative errors in quadrature.
    Numerator and denominator are sums over two INDEPENDENT samples
    (run_06 against run_12, different order), so there is no covariance
    to subtract either.  The independence needs no argument beyond the
    orders being different, and the n(w<0) test of "ARE THE SAMPLES
    PAIRED" agrees at once: 14 273 for the reference against 0 for LO,
    the LO matrix element being positive definite.
  So the delta-method bar used by the sum pane and the 2x2 is on
    NOTHING drawn on this figure.  That is not an oversight: it belongs
    to a ratio whose two sums run over ONE set of events, and no
    quantity on this canvas is one.  Where it does apply to this
    comparison is the component-FRACTION double ratio at the foot of
    this section, which is printed precisely so that the two error
    treatments of the same statement can be read against each other.

--- WHICH z IS THE FIRST INDEX, and why Z_0Z_T and Z_TZ_0 are not the
--- same curve on Delta phi(e+e-) ---
The two mixed components are NOT interchangeable here, and the figure
shows it.  z1_ch is 11 and z2_ch is 13 on ALL 250 000 events of BOTH
samples -- the extractor reads each z's channel off the event record --
so the FIRST z is always the one that went to e+e-.  ms_pol_23.0_23.T
is therefore (electron-z longitudinal, muon-z transverse) and
ms_pol_23.T_23.0 the other way round.  Delta phi(e+e-) is built from
the electron z alone, so it is directly sensitive to which of the two
the first index names, and the two components have genuinely different
shapes in it within ONE sample: the last-bin/first-bin ratio of the
normalised Delta phi spectrum is 15.7 for Z_0Z_T against 49.1 for
Z_TZ_0 at LO, and 14.0 against 20.6 at NLO.  M(e+ mu+) uses one lepton
from each z and is nearly symmetric between them, which is why their
K-factors agree there (1.360 / 1.348) and not here (1.378 / 1.354).

That asymmetry is the whole of the largest excursion on either panel 6:
K(Z_TZ_0) = 2.94 +- 0.18 in the lowest Delta phi bin against ~1.3 for
everything else.  It is not a statistical accident -- 3547 NLO and 3308
LO selected events in that bin -- and the mechanism is plain in the
numbers above: with the electron z TRANSVERSE the LO spectrum is very
nearly empty at small Delta phi (it needs a boosted z, which LO 2 -> 2
kinematics supplies only in the tail), and real radiation at NLO fills
a region that started almost empty.  A large K on a small denominator
is what a fixed-order ratio does at the edge of LO phase space; it says
the LO prediction is unreliable there, not that the NLO one is large.

SCALE UNCERTAINTY -- THE CONVENTIONS, STATED, AND BOTH ALTERNATIVES
==========================================================================

WHERE THE COLUMNS CAME FROM.  They were NOT cached.  Before this pass
data/weights.npz and data/weights_LO.npz carried w_MUR1.0_MUF1.0 and
no other scale point, so the eight remaining MUR*_MUF* columns could
not be summed and were not approximated.  Both files were re-read from
their HepMC by extract_hepmc_pol.py with KEEP_WEIGHTS widened to all
nine -- 32.1 s for run_06_decayed_1 and 31.9 s for run_12_decayed_1,
one streaming pass each.  EVERY COLUMN THAT WAS ALREADY THERE CAME
BACK BIT-FOR-BIT IDENTICAL; the two .npz grew by the eight new columns
and by nothing else, which is why every figure and every number of the
earlier passes is unchanged.  The three other spinmode samples were
not re-read: nothing on these two figures uses them.

*** THE TWO LABELS ARE TRANSPOSED IN THESE FILES. ***
The weight the HepMC N line calls MURa_MUFb is the one generated at
muR = b, muF = a.  The two factors are right; which scale each belongs
to is swapped.  Measured three ways, all on these files:
  (1) p p > z z at order = LO is the Born, O(alpha_s^0), so its cross
      section is EXACTLY muR independent and can only move with muF.
      In weights_LO.npz the weight is degenerate in the SECOND label on
      all 250 000 events and varies only with the FIRST.  So the first
      label is the factorisation scale.
  (2) Directly, on event 1 of run_12_decayed_1.  Its LHE <rwgt> gives
      ids 1001-1003 (muR = 1, 2, 0.5 at muF = 1) the same value
      2.3950370e-02, ids 1004-1006 (muF = 2) 2.3914174e-02 and ids
      1007-1009 (muF = 0.5) 2.3803324e-02.  The HepMC of the same
      event has 2.3803324e-02 under all three MUR0.5_* names,
      2.3950370e-02 under MUR1.0_* and 2.3914174e-02 under MUR2.0_*.
  (3) The mechanism.  The LHE header writes the nine with muF OUTER
      and muR INNER, each cycling (1.0, 2.0, 0.5); the name generator
      assumes muR outer and muF inner with the same cycle.  Composing
      the two is exactly a transposition and it reproduces all nine.
IT CHANGES NO NUMBER HERE.  An envelope is a min and a max over a SET,
and a permutation of a set moves neither.  The 7-point subset is
defined by which two points it DROPS, so it is not automatically safe
-- but it is: the pair dropped, (muR, muF) = (0.5, 2.0) and (2.0,
0.5), is the anti-diagonal, which the transposition maps onto itself.
What it does change is the reading of any INDIVIDUAL point, so every
per-point table below is printed under the TRUE muR / muF.

DECISION 1 -- WHICH ENVELOPE.  Drawn: 7-point: the nine muR/muF combinations minus the two non-adjacent ones, (muR, muF) = (0.5, 2.0) and (2.0, 0.5).
  The two dropped points put a factor of four between muR and muF,
  which manufactures a large log(muR/muF) the fixed-order calculation
  was never asked to resum; they inflate the envelope without probing
  a missing higher order.  Both envelopes are printed below.  On these
  samples the choice matters in one place and not in the other: on a
  single sample's cross section the two dropped points ARE the two
  extremes, and on the K-factor they are interior and the two
  envelopes agree to four decimals.

DECISION 2 -- CORRELATION BETWEEN NUMERATOR AND DENOMINATOR.
  FRACTIONS (component / full inside ONE sample): no choice, and the
    code offers none.  The same event weights appear top and bottom,
    so the envelope is of the RATIO recomputed scale by scale, NOT the
    ratio of two separately built envelopes.  The latter would be
    describing a configuration that cannot occur -- the numerator at
    its high scale while the denominator sits at its low one, on the
    same events.  Nearly all the variation cancels this way; what
    survives is the reweighting of the phase-space MIX inside the bin,
    which is why these bands are sub-percent where a single cross
    section moves by several.
  K-FACTOR: a real choice, both defensible, and they differ.
    DRAWN: the same muR/muF point in the NLO and the LO sample, envelope of the per-scale ratio.
    ALTERNATIVE, quoted in every table below but not drawn: independent envelopes on the two cross sections, combined as [num_min/den_max, num_max/den_min].
    The correlated one is drawn because the two samples are the same
    process, the same PDF set, the same functional scale choice and
    the same run card, so a scale is a physical setting shared between
    them rather than two independent nuisance parameters; and the
    muF / parton-luminosity part of the variation, which has nothing
    to do with the ORDER, then largely cancels.  The uncorrelated one
    is about a third wider.

DECISION 3 -- HOW A POLARISED COMPONENT IS VARIED AT ALL.
  the ms_pol_* weights exist at the central scale only, so a component is varied by the FULL weight's per-event ratio w_s/w_central -- the per-event polarisation fraction is held fixed.  Exact at LO, an approximation at NLO.
  Exact at LO because the muF and alpha_s dependence multiplies the
  whole squared matrix element at a fixed phase-space point,
  identically for the polarised projection and for the total.  An
  APPROXIMATION at NLO: the virtual and real pieces are not decomposed
  by polarisation, and this study's own result is that the components
  have different K-factors, which is the same statement as their
  alpha_s dependence differing.  The NLO component bands are therefore

DOES THE POLARISATION DEPENDENCE OF K SURVIVE THE SCALE BAND?
==========================================================================
K itself moves by -4.6 % / +6.8 % (correlated, 7-point) and the spread
BETWEEN components is about 7.5 %, so on the raw K the band and the
effect are the same size.  That is the wrong comparison and it is the
one RESULTS.md flagged.  The scale moves all five K TOGETHER -- it is
very nearly a common multiplicative shift -- so the quantity to test
is the RATIO of one component's K to the unpolarised K,

    D = K_component / K_full   ( == f_NLO / f_LO, the double ratio )

taken scale by scale so the common movement cancels.  D = 1 is "this
component has the same K as the total".
