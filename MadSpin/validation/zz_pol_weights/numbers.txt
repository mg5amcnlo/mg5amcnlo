--- IS THE DECAY CARD EXCLUSIVE IN EFFECT? (measured, not assumed) ---
The MadSpin card of every run is
  decay z > e+ e-
  decay z > mu+ mu-
which is exclusive in INTENT.  Whether it is exclusive in EFFECT is a
different question and it decides what these figures mean, so it is
read off the event record -- mark each z's end vertex, follow the chain
of status-44 copies, take the particles born at the final vertex -- for
every event of every file:

   spinmode        events one z->e+e- AND   at least one      other
                          other z->mu+mu-        z->e+e-   channels
   madspin         250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   onshell         250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   PA              250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0
   madspin_v1      250000   250000   100%   250000   100%          0

It is exclusive in effect: every event of every file has one z to e+e-
and the other to mu+mu-, with no other channel present at all.  So
BOTH observables have the full 250 000 events behind them before the
fiducial cuts, and the sparsity that made M(e+ mu+) undrawable on the
earlier decay z > light light samples (312 of 250 000 survivors) is
gone.

WHAT THE CROSS SECTIONS ARE OF.  Because the card is exclusive, the
sigma quoted for each file is
     sigma(p p > z z) x BR(z -> e+e-) x BR(z -> mu+mu-) x 2
(the 2 being the two ways of assigning the channels to the two z), and
NOT an inclusive p p > z z cross section.  The earlier inclusive
samples quoted ~13.1 pb, essentially the production cross section
because decay z > light light has a branching fraction of ~1; these
quote ~0.031 pb, smaller by ~2.35e-03, which is that product of
branching fractions.  The two sets of numbers are NOT comparable as
rates and only the ratios between the modes are.

--- DO THE SAMPLES SHARE PRODUCTION EVENTS? ---
This decides the error bar on every mode-to-mode comparison in this
file, so it is established and not assumed.  If the runs had
decayed one common set of production events, the mode-to-mode ratio
would be CORRELATED, a paired error would be correct, and it would
be much smaller than the quadrature one.  They did not.

spinmode        events     n(w < 0)   sigma_tot pb corr(m_4l) row by row
madspin         250000        14273       0.030874    --  (reference)
onshell         250000        13962       0.032415 -0.00028 +- 0.00203
PA              250000        14099       0.031116 +0.00041 +- 0.00203
madspin_v1      250000        14179       0.030919 -0.00005 +- 0.00203

n(w < 0) is the DECISIVE column and it is permutation invariant.
Neither MadSpin nor Pythia can change the sign of an event weight --
MadSpin multiplies by a positive decay factor and Pythia passes the
LHE weight through -- so the number of negative-weight events is a
property of the PRODUCTION sample alone and cannot depend on the
spinmode or on the order the events are written in.  Two decays of
one production sample would give the same count EXACTLY.  These
differ, so a common production sample is excluded outright, in any
order, and not merely left unproven.

corr(m_4l) corroborates it and is the nearest thing the cached
columns have to the sqrt(shat) the sibling m_tt study paired on.
For a 2 -> 2 production event the four-lepton invariant mass IS the
production m(ZZ), so two decays of one production event would agree
in it to the dressing; row by row it is consistent with ZERO on
~243 000 common rows, where pairing would give ~1.

The .npz carries no production-level column at all -- no
sqrt(shat), no event number, no production kinematics -- so the
literal "max |Delta sqrt(shat)| = 0" test of the m_tt study cannot
be run on this cache.  It does not need to be: the negative-weight
count is a stronger test in the direction that matters, because it
is permutation invariant, and it FAILS.

CONSEQUENCE: every between-mode error in this file and on the
figures is the PLAIN quadrature sum, and that is CORRECT here and
not merely conservative.  A paired bar would be wrong, not just
optimistic.  (Within a single sample the covariance is kept and
must be -- see "integrated polarisation ratios" and
pol_analysis.shape_density.)

--- THE SHAPE RATIO: what the variant-B pane draws ---
   (1/sigma dsigma/dX)_Y / (1/sigma dsigma/dX)_madspin
for every Y in one pane -- Y = onshell, Y = PA and, on this variant
alone, Y = madspin_v1.  Each mode is divided by
its OWN cross section before the division, so the RATE difference
between the modes divides out and what is left is the SHAPE.  That is
the pane this data wants: onshell differs from madspin in rate (+4.99 %
inclusive) and much less in shape, PA agrees in rate (1.4 sigma) and
differs in shape.  A ratio still carrying the normalisation shows the
first as a large flat offset and buries the second underneath it.

WHICH SIGMA NORMALISES EACH CURVE.  Two candidates, and they are not
the same number:
   selected  the whole selected fiducial cross section, every event
             passing the lepton cuts, INCLUDING those whose observable
             falls outside the drawn range;
   inrange   the integral of the drawn histogram, in-range events only.

THE FIGURES USE: inrange
   -- the integral of the drawn histogram (in-range events only)
because it makes the pane an exact statement about what is on the
canvas: the cross-section-weighted mean of the drawn ratio over the
drawn bins is then 1 by construction, so every visible departure from 1
is paid for by another visible bin.  With "selected" the out-of-range
rate -- which is mode dependent -- slides the whole pane by an amount
whose cause is off the canvas.  Both are tabulated below; the choice is
made for that reason and not because it changes an answer.

THE ERROR ON THIS PANE, and it is two different things multiplied
together.  WITHIN one sample the bin content and the sigma that
normalises it are sums over the SAME events -- the bin is a subset of
the normalisation -- so they are correlated and the delta-method error
of pol_analysis.ratio is used, exactly as for the polarisation ratios.
A plain sqrt(sum w^2) would overstate the bar by 1/sqrt(1 - 2 p) for a
bin holding a fraction p of the rate: 8 % on a twelfth of the Delta phi
rate, which would understate the chi2 by 14 %.  BETWEEN samples there
is NO correlation to keep -- see "DO THE SAMPLES SHARE PRODUCTION
EVENTS?" above -- so the two relative errors add in quadrature.  One
degree of freedom is removed from the chi2 because each curve was
divided by its own normalisation, which is exactly one constraint.

WHAT THE PANE SAYS ABOUT madspin_v1 -- the comparison this variant
was extended for.  madspin_v1 is MadSpin's LEGACY spin-correlation
path and the reference madspin sample is the current DENSITY
method, so this curve is legacy-against-density.  All four samples
are Pythia8-showered HepMC read by the same extractor with the same
selection and the same binning, so the curve is a SPINMODE
difference and nothing else -- there is no shower-versus-parton
confusion in it.

   m_epmup   chi2/ndf = 7.00/6 = 1.17   max|pull| 1.73
   dphi_ee   chi2/ndf = 10.27/11 = 0.93   max|pull| 1.64

That is a NULL result on both observables: no bin departs from 1 by
as much as 2 sigma and the chi2 per degree of freedom is about 1.
The legacy path and the density method agree in SHAPE on both
observables at these statistics, and they agree in RATE too -- the
inclusive cross sections differ by 0.15 % and the two fiducial
rates by 1.1 and 0.4 sigma; see "THE SPINMODES COMPARED" above.
This is the pane's answer and it is a different answer from the
other two curves on it: onshell differs in rate, PA differs in
shape, madspin_v1 differs in neither.  A null result is only worth
as much as its power, so read it next to the error column in the
madspin_v1 tables above: the bars are ~1 % per bin on M(e+ mu+)
and ~1-3 % on Delta phi, so a shape effect of the size PA shows
(up to 4 %) would have been seen, and one much below a percent
would not.

For the contrast, "THE SPINMODES COMPARED" above quotes a
"SHAPE only" chi2 as well.  It is a DIFFERENT statistic and the two
are not interchangeable: that one rescales the other mode to the
reference's SELECTED fiducial rate and keeps the plain sqrt(sum w^2)
per-bin bar, so it neither uses the in-range normalisation nor removes
the within-sample correlation.  Its bars are the larger and its chi2
the smaller of the two.  The pane draws the one computed here.

The sum over ALL events is 1 to within 0.017 % and 0.36 sigma.  That is
not a null result: the interference between different polarisations of
the same z is odd in the decay angles and integrates to zero over the
full angular phase space, so an unrestricted sum MUST come back to 1 and
the fact that it does is a check on the weights themselves.  The
interference is visible only differentially, or after a cut that
restricts the decay angles -- which every lepton selection does, and
which is why both selected rows above sit below 1 by many sigma where
the unrestricted one does not.
