AmpliCol MC@NLO efficiency review — 2026-10-08

Conclusion
==========
There is credible room to reduce physics evaluations through better stopping,
more useful retention of generation history, and better proposal training.
These are more promising than optimizing sampler arithmetic or increasing job
size alone. No speedup from a changed production algorithm has been measured
in this investigation; retrospective capacity and expired-trial fractions
must not be presented as achievable or additive CPU savings.

Preserve two stages, one physics channel per executable, the 3% absolute-rate
survey with at least four iterations, the 10% event reserve, folding rules,
native correction weights, and all existing strict 1% full-tail checks.
Proposals below concern setup/sampling inside this architecture.

Evidence and scope
==================
The two completed 300K ttbar samples use no folding, automatic req_acc,
seed 19727, five cores, and the same survey and numerical sources. Their
event-job limits are 2,500 and 30,000. The latter generation-only rerun
restored the original allocation order before allocating workers.

                                    2,500/job       30,000/job
Workers                                  135               13
Generation trials                  3,074,406        3,064,052
Generation CPU [s]                 3,093.497        2,941.371
Generation phase wall [s]             622.426          657.409
Final events / all trials              9.758%           9.791%
Final-ineligible generation trials     3.074%          13.679%
Reserve / still-eligible trials       11.077%          12.477%
Collected full overweight mass         0.576%           0.400%

Larger jobs reduced recorded generation CPU by 4.9% but barely changed the
number of trials and increased generation wall time by 5.6%. This is one
comparison, not a repeated timing experiment. Larger jobs have better yield
from eligible trials, offset by more history becoming ineligible for events.
Ineligible trials still contribute to integration estimates and training.

Instrumented, isolated replays of representative gg/GF3 workers:
  - Complete integrand: 86.4% and 87.8% of CPU.
  - All sampler bookkeeping: 1.13% and 0.77%.
  - Historical envelope reevaluation, included above: 0.43% and 0.12%.
  - Virtual-stream integrand, included in integrand: 0.39% and 0.37%.
A separate replay measured complete candidate preparation/writing at 10.79%.
The existing Write_events timer captures only about half this activity.
All three replays preserved candidate LHE and POOL4 bytes and all non-timing
res.dat numbers. Use within-replay fractions, not replay/original time ratios.
The integrand includes phase space, PDFs, subtraction and physics bookkeeping,
not just matrix-element arithmetic. The dominant gg/GF2 and GF3 channels
account for about 88% of the small-job generation CPU.

1. Improve the tail-aware point forecast and completion checks
============================================================
The current tail-failure forecast at simple_integrator.f90:2600 uses
  effective = min(survivors, 0.8*Q, Q*0.01/current_overweight).
This is a heuristic: overweight mass is a discrete nonlinear function of the
unweighting threshold. It can request substantial additional work after a
relatively small tail failure. Completion is checked only after the entire
nonzero-point batch; the last batches account for approximately a quarter of
all generation trials, although they are not all unnecessary.

Use the actual stored weight distribution to predict viable yield. For fixed
epoch normalizers M_k and storage cutoffs C_k, an admissible common normalized
threshold z obeys z >= max_k(C_k/M_k). Above these cutoffs the stored pool
contains every overweight contribution. Evaluate the observed full-weight
tail as a function of z; count saved-uniform survivors at the resulting
threshold and estimate additional points from the current epoch's acceptance.
Continue to include the effect of events about to expire from eligibility.

Initially change the forecast alone. Keep final quota, full-trial, reserve,
and worst-final-subset checks authoritative. The latter selection checks
contain discrete changes and must not all be assumed monotone in z.

Then separate completion-check frequency from map-update frequency: check
near predicted completion in short chunks with the current map fixed, and
stop the iteration when the quota plus every final check succeeds. Ensure
shortened-iteration counters/moments/metadata remain consistent. Retain the
required reserve rather than treating it as avoidable overshoot.

Offline evidence: holding recorded final maps/envelopes, eligible epochs,
cutoffs and final quotas fixed, the pools support these larger reserves:
  2,500/job: 330,092 -> 376,934 (+14.19%).
 30,000/job: 330,006 -> 389,857 (+18.14%).
All 148 adjusted pools pass the production native worker validator with all
three strict 1% checks. This is final-state capacity, not a causal earlier-stop
replay: the final envelopes use later information. It supports trying a better
forecast but does not establish 14–18% recoverable CPU time.

2. Stabilize adaptation and retain useful generation history
==========================================================
The production path adapts unfolded maps after every unfinished iteration,
including small finishing batches. Original AmpliCol conditions adaptation
on sufficient new statistics (more than 20% of accumulated statistics) and
remaining accuracy needs. Restore a statistical significance/stability
criterion or damping, using an unweighting-quality objective in generation.
Do not freeze all coordinates: retain adaptation for ifold=1 and preserve
the fixed folded-coordinate contract.

Only the last eight event-producing epochs currently contribute events.
This excludes 3.1% of small-job trials and 13.7% of large-job trials at final
selection. Explore keeping epochs according to their useful event mass,
proposal quality and storage-floor compatibility, or merging epochs with
identical proposals. Short completion checks should not automatically create
new proposal epochs or consume history slots. Keep all attempted points in
the integration estimate regardless of event eligibility.

Keeping everything indefinitely is not automatically best: a poor old map
or high storage cutoff can constrain the global selection threshold. Changes
need a consistent selection/normalization treatment and statistical validation.
The expired-trial percentages are not recoverable-savings estimates and must
not be added to the surplus percentages above.

3. Improve the common survey proposal and its training objective
===============================================================
Every archived ttbar survey map has only eight learned histogram bins per
coordinate. The 2,048 sampling cells interpolate those bins; they do not
represent 2,048 independently measured pieces of the distribution.
determine_sizefill at simple_integrator.f90:480 chooses the learned size.

A 3% integral uncertainty does not ensure a good proposal for unweighting.
Test 16/32 learned bins with sufficient statistics and smoothing. Avoid simply
increasing resolution with empty/noisy bins. The current strategy-3 grid
accumulator is an order-dependent one-sided soft maximum; robust/high-quantile
targets and damped updates may learn the typical envelope more reliably.
Any robustness applies to training, never to clipping physics contributions.

The shared survey costs about 60 CPU seconds versus 3,093 for generation.
Doubling that survey investment would pay off with approximately a 2%
generation improvement. This is a break-even calculation, not an observed
benefit. Extra proposal training remains inside stage one and retains its
minimum four iterations and 3% absolute-rate requirement. Compare changes
to resolution, training rule and survey effort separately.

4. Revisit relative epoch envelopes
==================================
Original AmpliCol compute_fmax uses a top-5%-candidate quantile. The native
adapter's native_update_envelopes at simple_integrator.f90:2374 uses a maximum
over counterfactually reweighted stored candidates. A rare point can therefore
alter the relative utilization of different epochs substantially.

Test robust relative normalizers, keeping exact importance factors and the
actual 1% full-tail checks. Merely lowering every envelope by the same factor
does nothing: that common factor cancels against the normalized rank threshold.
Consequently maximum/integral ratios are not predicted acceptance gains, and
the original 5% quantile is not itself a replacement for the required 1%
cross-section tail criterion. This change interacts with history retention.

5. Separate internal virtual/nonvirtual proposals if needed
=========================================================
The virtual residual carries about 0.6–0.8% of the surveyed absolute rate.
Its inverse-stream-probability-weighted maximum nevertheless dominates the
initial common envelope in four of five channels. Direct virtual evaluation
is cheap here, but rare virtual draws may make shared sampling inefficient.

Possible improvements are separate internal maps/envelopes or stream
probabilities chosen using tail behavior and evaluation cost. This still
means one physical channel per executable. It requires retaining stream
identity and birth probability in pool metadata and including the stream
probability ratio in historical reweighting. Existing coordinate-only history
cannot support arbitrary stream-probability adaptation correctly. Keep virtual
fits themselves frozen unless their changing absolute target is also handled.
The archived data do not establish the achievable gain. This is a more
intrusive second-round experiment, not the first implementation to try.

Other possibilities and limits
==============================
Candidate materialization is approximately 11% of one representative worker.
Around 63% of small-job stored candidates are unused in the final reserve.
Avoiding all of their preparation at negligible cost would have an idealized
ceiling around 7% overall, but future envelope changes require retaining the
necessary information and later reconstruction may erase the saving.

Caching unchanged historical Jacobians/envelopes is an exact cleanup whose
measured ceiling is small. Faster virtual amplitudes cannot provide a large
gain for these ttbar runs either. Increasing job size alone is not a solution
to trial efficiency and can reduce concurrency/load balance.

MINT's product bounding envelope is another proposal-training reference.
AmpliCol's factorized maps can in principle represent a product proposal;
porting that machinery does not itself solve correlations. More general
correlated/cell/surrogate proposals are possible but substantially more
intrusive and lack a quantified benefit from these diagnostics.

The saved MINT 300K sample used 2,419,293 trials (12.40% final/trial) versus
3,074,406 (9.76%) here. Matching its trial count would require about 21% fewer
AmpliCol trials. This is a useful optimization target, not a demonstrated
attainable gain. The uncertainty and per-worker overweight contracts also
differ, so those timings are not an equal-precision/tail-protocol comparison.

Validation and recommended sequence
===================================
No obvious probability-factor, missing-trial or duplicated-survey error was
found in the rate combination. Nevertheless the two job-size results differ
by 2.88 nominal quadrature errors in signed rate, and about 3.13 in absolute
rate. Shared survey/seeds mean the difference is not necessarily independent;
one pair neither establishes bias nor validates uncertainty calibration.
Quota/tail stopping changes require checking rates and shapes across seeds.

First implement only the tail-aware forecast, then completion checks separated
from adaptation. Next test stable adaptation/history retention and better
shared survey maps. Benchmark each individually before combining them.
Use fixed inputs/common survey for initial computational comparisons, then
fresh-survey independent seeds for statistical validation. Start with analytic
signed, sparse, narrow-tail and folded targets; measure bias, empirical spread,
quoted-error coverage and weighted shape moments. For the leading physics
candidate, use multiple independent moderate samples rather than just one
new 300K run. A fixed-trial diagnostic is a useful validation control, not a
third production integration stage.

Report trials, CPU, wall time, final signed effective events, tail fractions,
and integration errors. Preserve correction factors and never estimate the
rate from retained candidates alone. The empirical 1% checks measure observed
tail mass; they do not guarantee the absence of unseen rare phase space.
