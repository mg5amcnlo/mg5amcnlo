\subsection{Sampled native components and a Delta-free veto}
\label{sec:delta-sampled}

Explicit summation is not required by MC@NLO. One may sample native
history groups and Born colour flows, provided the same physical
partitions and importance weights are used consistently in its source
terms. The relevant settings are
\begin{verbatim}
#MCExplicitKLSum
.false.
#MCSubtractionAtFixedFlow
.true.
#FKSExplicitSum
.false.
\end{verbatim}
These settings remove the mixture of differently damped histories from
the sign of a single sampled H component. Changing the card alone does
not supply the information needed for later matching. The new source
writer retains a component $a=(h,d,c)$: native history/sector $h$,
Born integration diagram $d$, and Born colour flow $c$. The source also
samples starting-scale and colour-insertion auxiliaries $\lambda$.
Its H real,
MC and G rows are combined and certified within that component before
unweighting. The S sample retains $d,c$, while its original integration
owner is only provenance. Its actual first shower branching determines
$h$; all eligible radiators can compete.

The diagram label matters because the source's real and counterterm
contributions use diagram fractions at their corresponding real-projected
and underlying Born points. A globally summed evaluator that omits this
partition is not a local component density. Likewise the physical Born
flow fraction must remain, although the inverse probability used to
sample that flow is not divided into the physical density a second time.
Fixed gluon flow still has two colour connections.

With all physical partitions understood, let
\begin{equation}
 H_{0,a}=R_a-D_{G,a},\qquad
 H_{\Delta,a}(\lambda)=\Delta_a(\lambda) H_{0,a},\qquad
 u_a=\pos{-H_{0,a}}.
\end{equation}
The implemented sampled-$\Delta$ pair is
\begin{equation}
 v_{S,a}=\min\!\left(\frac{u_a}{K_a},1\right),\qquad
 a_{H^-,a}=\max\!\left(1-\frac{K_a}{u_a},0\right),\qquad
 H_{-,\mathrm{rem},a}=\Delta_a\pos{u_a-K_a}.
 \label{eq:sampled-delta-pair}
\end{equation}
The source event weights are unchanged. For $u_a=0$ there is no S
rejection; for $K_a=0$ the operational rule keeps the S event and the
negative H component, making no unsupported transfer. No-emission and
protected-threshold S events retain their existing treatment.
The local evaluator replays unit $\Delta$ in the selected component,
preserving the complete G source algebra while avoiding a numerical
source-Sudakov evaluation. Ordinary sources instead retain their
bogus-Sudakov profile and use
$u_a^{\rm ord}=\pos{P_a(D_{G,a}-R_a)}$ in both decisions.

The S route uses the exact source Born provider and the saved shower
ancestry. An exchange of identical Born legs requires transforming
both the configured diagram and the oriented colour-flow basis.
The generated registry contains interaction trees and colour topologies
to certify that transformation. Multiple gluon daughter orientations
are marked with probabilities $K_a/\sum_{a'}K_{a'}$, not with FKS
partition functions. Native inverse-Born kinematics and incoming
fractions are checked against the actual pre-shuffle S state.

Identical-particle multiplicities require a common change of measure.
After removing the reduced source's outer multiplicity
$F=\mathrm{fac}_i\mathrm{fac}_j$, its native MC kernel still contains
$I_B/I_R$. Multiplication of all local $R,D,K$ by $I_R/I_B$ gives the
measure per actual Born emitter. The reduced source represents
$O=F I_B/I_R$ equivalent emitters; the worker checks that $O$ is a
positive integer. For $g\to gg$ with $m$ identical outgoing
Born gluons, $I_R/I_B=m+1$, $F=m(m+1)$ and $O=m$.
The common factor leaves the local ratios unchanged.

This construction resolves the mixed-component negative-part problem,
but not every finite-shower difference. In a positive single-component
description with actual S emission density
$\beta_a\Delta_{{\rm PS},a}K_a$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sampled-delta-pair}
changes the endpoint density by
\begin{equation}
 \delta\rho_a=
 (\overline\Delta_a-\beta_a\Delta_{{\rm PS},a})\min(u_a,K_a),
 \qquad
 \overline\Delta_a=\mathbb E_{\lambda\mid q,a}[\Delta_a(\lambda)].
\end{equation}
The average uses the source auxiliary law before H unweighting; the
algorithm does not need to evaluate it. The local undamped numerator
and kernel are independent of those auxiliaries.
Exact cancellation requires the corresponding equality of measures;
equal source and shower factors and $\beta_a=1$ are sufficient in this
idealization. They are not assumed. Signed S contributions, different
starting-scale laws, kernel correspondence and later continuations
still matter. The literal $(1-R_a/K_a)_+$ follows in the special case
$D_{G,a}=K_a$; more generally the removable negative parts must agree.
The G/spin completion need not satisfy that condition.
